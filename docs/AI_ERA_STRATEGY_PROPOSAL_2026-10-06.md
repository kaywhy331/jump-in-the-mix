## Summary: users, not AI, will decide Jump in the Mix

Jump in the Mix's future will be decided by whether real owners use it, not by artificial intelligence: on 2026-10-05 production held **2 users, 1 workspace and 4 contacts**, no outside tester had been admitted, no analytics collector existed and nothing could be bought (VERIFIED-DOC, VERIFIED-RUNTIME). The AI market itself leaves the product a defensible opening. Phone and assistant AI is commoditizing the *wording* of follow-up (drafting, summarizing, recalling one person's context), but every shipped platform feature reacts to conversations that are already open, none keeps a plan of who is due for which step, and third-party apps still cannot send texts on their own: on iPhone they can only prefill a message for the person to send, and on Android they must become the default texting app to do more. The six verdicts follow from that. The niche survives and will still be used for relationship follow-ups, but the company survives only if its first owners activate and keep coming back within roughly two quarters. It should position on the owner's own number, the owner's own voice and a next step for every contact, not on "AI" or "no AI". Phone-local AI shrinks the drafting value and leaves the planning, record-keeping and handoff value intact. Marketing should be founder-led in owner communities and built on public, reusable follow-up plans rather than paid ads. And the web app should keep its trust core while pivoting from a broad template library to a next-step engine, adding owner-reviewed AI suggestions and an assistant connector only if Kevin changes the standards that now bar them. Autonomous agents are compounding fast (on METR's software tasks the length of task an agent finishes half the time has doubled every 89–131 days, depending on the start year), yet on real paid freelance projects the best agent delivered client-acceptable work only **15.8%** of the time in July 2026 ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/); [CAIS](https://safe.ai/blog/significant-increase-in-digital-labor-automation)), so supervised copilots, not autopilots, remain the realistic pattern for small-business customer contact through 2030. None of this was tested with customers, and the shared web-search budget ran out partway through the research, so several market figures rest on direct page fetches or search summaries.

## A hardened, AI-free app with two users and no measurements

Jump in the Mix is far more built than used. The audited checkout holds **93 pages, 52 API routes and 118 database models**, including a 30-page staff console with six roles (VERIFIED-CODE). The core loop is short and sound: add a person or log what happened, start a Mix or schedule one follow-up, review it on Today, open the text or email app or place the call, then record what happened and the next promise (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/standards/01-product-concept-and-goals.md:72-77`). Around that loop sit a Journey map; five ready-made Mega-Mixes for contractors, real-estate agents, consultants, photographers and recruiters (the real-estate set was rebuilt on 2026-09-29 as 16 Mixes and 28 Beats); a Marketplace; a calendar; Quick Add; phone-contact import; lead intake from nine source kinds; review and referral pages; and waitlist waves with personal invitations (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/MEGA_MIXES_2026-09-22.md:9-15`, `docs/REALTOR_JOURNEY_IMPLEMENTATION_2026-09-29.md:18-20`; VERIFIED-CODE, `src/lib/intake-types.ts`). The engineering is defensive by constitution, which lists timezone-safe scheduling, idempotent outcomes, durable worker leases, undo, append-only history, duplicate-safe imports and encrypted backups as strengths that must not be weakened (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/standards/00-constitution.md:47-56`). It was built at extraordinary speed by one founder working with AI coding agents: **75 merged pull requests since 2026-09-01, 42 tagged production releases in 15 days, and 271 Claude co-author trailers across 374 non-merge commits** (VERIFIED-RUNTIME, read-only `gh`; VERIFIED-CODE, `git log`). Set against a single production workspace, that breadth is building ahead of demand (INFERRED).

At runtime the product contains **no AI at all**. `package.json` lists no language-model, speech or generative package, the lockfile holds none, and a repository-wide grep finds only developer tooling (VERIFIED-CODE, `package.json:82-100`). Dictation is the browser's own speech recognition, and the Today read-aloud is the browser's speech synthesis (VERIFIED-CODE, `src/components/VoiceNoteButton.tsx:28-34`, `src/components/TodayBriefing.tsx:14-30`). Drafts are authored templates with merge fields, and each Beat's text, email and call versions come from deterministic regular-expression rules (VERIFIED-CODE, `src/lib/variant-generation.ts:3-7`). ## Agents compound fast but still fail at accountable customer work

Capability is compounding on a schedule few technologies have matched. METR's January 2026 update puts the doubling time of the task length an agent completes with 50% success at **130.8 days since 2023 and 88.6 days since 2024**, with Claude Opus 4.5 at about 320 minutes [independent] ([METR, Time Horizon 1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)). METR's Senate testimony on 2026-09-30 cited a longer-run trend of a 7-month doubling [independent] ([METR testimony](https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-30-chris-painter-senate-testimony/)). Stanford's AI Index 2026 shows agents on OSWorld, a benchmark of real computer tasks, rising from about 12% to **66.3%** in a single year while still failing about one attempt in three on structured benchmarks [independent] ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report)). Extrapolating the faster trend from Opus 4.5 gives agents that handle about a work-week of software tasks by late 2026 and about six weeks by late 2027; even the slower trend reaches multi-week projects before 2030 (INFERRED, the research team's extrapolation; METR itself calls measurements above 16 hours unreliable).

The reliability gap is the central fact for a follow-up product. On the Remote Labor Index, 240 real paid freelance projects, the best agent produced client-acceptable work **2.5%** of the time at the October 2025 launch and **15.8%** by July 2026, and automated AI judges overstated quality by roughly two to three times [independent] ([CAIS](https://safe.ai/blog/significant-increase-in-digital-labor-automation); [RLI paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26787)). Salesforce's own CRMArena-Pro found leading agents at about 58% success on single-turn CRM tasks, falling to about **35%** on multi-turn ones, with "near-zero inherent confidentiality awareness" [vendor-run research] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18878)). Writing one personal follow-up is a single-turn job that frontier models have done well since 2024; carrying a customer relationship across many turns without supervision is where errors compound (INFERRED). The market has already punished autopilots. TechCrunch reported a former employee's estimate that the AI sales-agent startup 11x had about $3M of real retained revenue against $14M of claimed ARR, with staff describing "losing 70-80% of customers" [independent press] ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have/)). Gartner forecasts that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 and estimates that only about 130 of the thousands of vendors claiming agentic products offer real agentic features [forecast] ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)).

The agent products themselves keep changing shape. OpenAI reportedly retired "ChatGPT agent" in August 2026 [reported; OpenAI's help page returned 403] ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/chatgpt-agent-mode/)). Google shut its standalone Project Mariner agent in May 2026 and moved the technology into Gemini and Chrome [shipped] ([Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/05/google-shuts-down-project-mariner-ai-agent.html)). OpenAI replaced its unprompted Pulse briefing with user-configured scheduled tasks on paid tiers, capped at once an hour and liable to pause when unattended [shipped] ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/17/openai-launches-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt-details-here/)). What persists is the move of agents into operating systems, browsers and general assistants that connect to systems of record: ChatGPT Work reaches "email, calendars, CRMs" through plugins [shipped] ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-chatgpt-work-agent-launch)), and Google's always-on Gemini Spark is a US beta for AI Ultra subscribers only [beta] ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/19/google-introduces-gemini-spark-a-24-7-agentic-assistant-with-gmail-integration/)). Features move down-market quickly: Gemini's Daily Brief launched on paid tiers in May 2026 and reached free US users by August–September ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-daily-brief-free-users-3709041/)), so today's top-tier agents should be expected on mass tiers within 6–18 months (INFERRED from one observed cycle).

Small firms adopt slowly, and mostly to augment their own work. The Census Bureau puts AI use at **17–20% of US firms** from December 2025 to May 2026, with no significant change among firms under 20 employees and firms of 1–4 employees under 20% [government] ([Census](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html)). Among users, 66% use AI only to augment tasks, and sales and marketing is the top function at 52% [government] ([Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/CES-WP-26-25.html)). Payment data are starker: JPMorgan Chase Institute found **17.7%** of small businesses paying for any AI service by December 2025, 15.3% of nonemployer firms and **8.9% in construction**, at a median of $28 a month [independent, transaction data] ([JPMorgan Chase Institute](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/understanding-ai-use-by-small-businesses)). McKinsey's 2026 survey reports agent scaling at 40% of organizations with more than $1B in revenue but flat at 22% in smaller ones [analyst; search summary] ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/the-state-of-ai-in-2026-on-the-road-to-roi-aug-2026.pdf)). The agent wave therefore reaches owner-operators mainly as features inside software they already pay for and through consumer assistants, not as agents they build or buy (INFERRED).

Software economics are shifting rather than collapsing. Microsoft's CEO predicted in December 2024 that "business applications will collapse in the AI era" because they are "CRUD databases with business logic" ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/hey-why-do-i-need-excel-microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-foresees-a-disruptive-agentic-ai-era-that-could-aggressively-collapse-saas-apps)). In early February 2026, Anthropic's Cowork plug-ins set off a sell-off in which the S&P 500 Software & Services index fell nearly 13% over five sessions [independent press] ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-software-stocks-hit-by-anthropic-wakeup-call-on-ai-disruption-4485524)). Forrester calls the "death of SaaS" story overstated, forecasts SaaS spending rising from $318B in 2025 to $576B in 2029, and expects horizontal point tools with low switching costs to struggle while vertical tools fare better [analyst] ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/saas-as-we-know-it-is-dead-how-to-survive-the-saas-pocalypse/)). Pricing is moving from seats to actions and outcomes, for example $0.10 per standard Agentforce action and $0.99 per Intercom Fin outcome [shipped, vendor pricing] ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/); [Fin](https://fin.ai/pricing)). Epoch AI measured the price of reaching a fixed capability level falling 9x to 900x a year [independent] ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends)), while Gartner forecasts inference costs per agentic workflow rising more than fivefold through 2028 [forecast; search-result text, page returned 403] ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-17-gartner-predicts-ai-inference-costs-per-agentic-workflow-will-increase-more-than-fivefold-through-2028)). AI that prepares and ranks is nearly free next to a $15 plan; autonomous multi-step work is where costs climb (INFERRED).

Two rails matter for a small app. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) has become the shared way agents call tools: the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation launched in December 2025 citing more than 10,000 published MCP servers and use by Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot [shipped] ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-the-formation-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation)). Microsoft's Copilot Studio documentation states the corollary bluntly: "If a person can use an app or website, computer use can too", so lacking an API no longer keeps agents out [vendor] ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/computer-use)). The second rail runs the other way: consumer agents now contact businesses. Gemini on the Pixel 11 "can call businesses on users' behalf for table reservations or appointments" and added Angi and Thumbtack as connected apps [shipped, US] ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/pixel-11-has-few-hardware-changes-and-more-gemini/)), and trade coverage of Google I/O 2026 reports AI Mode phoning home-repair businesses for prices and availability from summer 2026 [announced; secondary] ([MarketingCode](https://www.marketingcode.com/mobile-ai-google-agentic-calling-home-repairs-ai-to-ai-booking-jul-2026/)). Customers accept this only conditionally: 87% say a company using generative AI for service must offer access to a human [analyst survey] ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-04-gartner-survey-finds-87-percent-of-customers-say-companies-using-genai-for-customer-service-must-provide-access-to-a-human-agent0)).

Regulation keeps autonomous outreach expensive and touches an owner sending one message at a time far less. The table condenses the agent-trajectory research note's (note 02) comparison; it is a synthesis, not legal advice.

| Outreach layer | When the app or an AI sends | When the owner sends from their own phone apps |
|---|---|---|
| Calls | AI-generated voices are "artificial" under the TCPA, so calls need prior express consent, written for telemarketing [enacted, FCC 2024-02-08] ([FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-400393A1.pdf)); an AI-disclosure rule has been pending since August 2024 [pending; secondary] ([Compliance Journal](https://compliancejournal.news/fcc-tcpa-ai-generated-calls-proposed-rules-2026/)) | A live human call does not trigger the artificial-voice rules; for telephone solicitations, do-not-call lists, calling hours and opt-outs still apply ([47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)) |
| Texts | Carrier A2P 10DLC registration and filtering; vendor blogs report unregistered traffic blocked since February 2025, though Twilio's documentation mentions only extra fees [industry rule] ([MessageDesk](https://www.messagedesk.com/blog/a2p-10dlc); [Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc)) | Handset traffic within typical-consumer patterns avoids registration, but the carriers' association still classes a business owner as a "Non-Consumer" sender [industry standard] ([CTIA](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf)) |
| Email | CAN-SPAM, plus authentication, DMARC and a 0.3% spam-rate ceiling for senders above 5,000 a day to Gmail ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business); [Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)) | CAN-SPAM still covers commercial content, but one-to-one mail stays far below bulk thresholds |
| AI disclosure | State bot laws, such as New Jersey's "at the start" disclosure for real-estate sales, and EU AI Act Article 50 from 2026-08-02 [enacted] ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/01/ai-disclosure-laws-on-chatbots-are-on-the-rise-key-takeaways-for-companies); [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/september/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-go-live)) | A message the owner reviews and sends as their own is arguably not an AI interacting with the consumer (INFERRED) |

The gap is narrowing for texts. The FCC narrowed its "revoke-all" consent rule on 2026-09-30, and the 7th Circuit held in July 2026 that texts are not "calls" for private do-not-call suits, splitting with the 9th Circuit [legal analysis] ([Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/insights/102o48j/fcc-narrows-the-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-permits-exclusive-opt-out-methods-and-se); [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/seventh-circuit-rules-text-messages-are-not-telephone-calls-under-tcpa-%C2%A7-227c5-a-circuit-split-emerges/)). AI voice remains the clearest line.

Four futures through 2030 follow from this evidence (INFERRED, the research team's synthesis, not probability-weighted). In the first, **capable but supervised**, which sits closest to current evidence, capability keeps compounding but trust, compliance and multi-turn reliability keep customer contact at micro-firms supervised; a human-sends app thrives if it becomes the owner's daily system of record and agents can call it. In **assistant absorption**, phone and browser assistants read the owner's messages, calls and calendar and propose who to follow up with, squeezing thin apps; survival then requires proprietary plan and outcome history and being the tool those assistants call rather than the one they replace. In **autopilot normalization**, multi-turn reliability passes roughly 80–90% and carrier and FCC frameworks for AI messages settle; human-sends becomes a premium niche or must add optional, consented automation for low-stakes messages. In **backlash**, agent incidents of the kind METR described to the Senate bring disclosure mandates and liability for unsupervised outreach, and the human-sends model gains.

## Phone AI is taking the wording job and leaving the plan

For a trades owner whose customer conversations are texts and calls, today's assistants have little to reason over.

"Local" AI is only partly local. Apple's on-device model has about 3 billion parameters compressed to 2 bits per weight and is "not designed to be a chatbot for general world knowledge" [vendor] ([Apple Machine Learning Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/apple-foundation-models-2025-updates)). Its context window is 4,096 tokens on iOS 26 (8,192 on iOS 27 for newer devices, per a session summary), against 32,768 for the Private Cloud Compute model [vendor] ([Apple WWDC26 session 319](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/319/)). On-device models are good at drafting, summarizing and pulling action items out of a message. A 4K–8K window cannot hold months of quotes, calls and job history, so long-horizon help depends on cloud models tied to one person's account (INFERRED).

The platform rules favor exactly the handoff Jump in the Mix already uses, and they favor native apps for everything else. Apple's message composer lets an app prefill a text, but it is presented "for a person's approval" and the Messages app does the sending [vendor documentation] ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/messageui/mfmessagecomposeviewcontroller)). Every sanctioned AI hook is native or experimental: App Intents for Siri; Apple's Foundation Models, whose Private Cloud Compute tier is free for App Store apps with fewer than two million downloads; Android AppFunctions, experimental and with Gemini integration in private preview as of May 2026; and WebMCP, in a Chrome origin trial ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/09/apple-outlines-major-ai-and-developer-tool-updates/); [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/ai/appfunctions); [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/all-the-news-from-the-google-io-2026-developer-keynote/)). On iPhone, web push works only after the user adds the site to the Home Screen and taps to allow notifications [shipped] ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). The `sms:` handoff is therefore the platform norm, not a workaround, while a web-only product stays invisible to Siri AI's app actions and Apple's semantic index (INFERRED).

### Silence, not wording, is the biggest measured loss

The largest documented failure in follow-up is no follow-up at all. Secret shoppers found 48% of real-estate buyer inquiries unanswered in 2014 [consultancy; the co-sponsor had a commercial interest] ([WAV Group](https://www.wavgroup.com/2014/01/13/agent-responsiveness-study-reveals-critical-flaws-in-real-estate-lead-response/)) and 47% in 2024, when **62% of open-house visitors got no follow-up** [independent; small sample] ([Secret Shopping, 2024](https://www.mikedp.com/articles/2024/8/13/secret-shopping-47-of-online-property-inquiries-are-ignored)). Only 20% of home-service owners reply to leads within an hour, and 80% say they are fully or nearly fully booked [vendor survey, Jobber, n=1,050] ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/)). Past relationships are where the money is: 66% of sellers used a referred or previous agent and 91% of buyers would use or recommend theirs, yet only 15% of buyers and 29% of sellers actually used a previous agent, after a median of 11 years in the home [trade association] ([NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers](https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf)). In home services, 59% of owners call referrals and repeat work their top lead sources [vendor survey] ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/)). A paid Google Local Services lead cost about $53 in February 2026 and about $233 per paying customer [agency data, one agency's clients] ([SearchLight Digital](https://searchlightdigital.io/google-local-service-ads-cost-per-lead/)), so one recovered job a month would pay for a $15 tool many times over (INFERRED). Two widely repeated statistics, "80% of sales need five follow-ups" and "44% of salespeople give up after one", have no rigorous source and should never appear in Jump in the Mix's copy ([Automation Experience](https://automationexperience.net/blog/the-follow-up-statistic-its-own-author-deleted/); [Conciergr](https://conciergr.com/blog/follow-up-statistics-sales)).

### Suspected AI authorship costs trust in relationship messages

The peer-reviewed evidence agrees that a known or suspected AI author carries a penalty, largest in emotional and relationship messages. AI responses can make people feel more heard, but the effect shrinks once they are labeled as AI ([PNAS 2024, "AI can help people feel heard, but an AI label diminishes this impact"](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319112121)). Disclosing AI use lowered trust across thirteen experiments, and being exposed by a third party was worse ([Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 2025, "The transparency dilemma: How AI disclosure erodes trust"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2025.104405)). Believing that an emotional marketing message was AI-written cut word of mouth and loyalty, less so for factual messages or when AI only edited ([Journal of Business Research 2025, "The AI-authorship effect"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2024.114984)). AI help in maintaining a relationship lowered the effort recipients perceived ([Journal of Social and Personal Relationships 2023, "Artificial intelligence and perceived effort in relationship maintenance"](https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075231189899)). The penalty concerns perceived authorship and effort, not text quality: undisclosed AI text often matches human text ([Judgment and Decision Making 2023, "Human favoritism, not AI aversion"](https://doi.org/10.1017/jdm.2023.37)). Pew finds that 76% of Americans say it matters to know whether content came from AI, 53% doubt they could tell, and 50% are more concerned than excited about AI, up from 37% in 2021 [independent survey] ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)). In housing, trust in AI to help find a home fell from 30% to 16% in a year ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/05/09/consumers-increasingly-wary-of-ais-role-in-homebuying)). Owners behave accordingly: home-service owners use AI for quoting (54%) and business writing (51%) far more than for customer communication (35%) [vendor survey] ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/)), and 7% of Realtors use chatbots for client communication against 46% who use AI-generated content [trade association] ([NAR 2025 Technology Survey](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf)).

| Message type | Preference for the owner's own voice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First response to a new lead | Eroding | Speed dominates, and 53% of homeowners are comfortable with AI handling initial inquiries [vendor survey] ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-customer-service-report-trends-statistics/)) |
| Estimate or quote chase | Stable to mixed | Factual messages carry a smaller penalty; a note from the person who quoted adds accountability |
| Post-job check-in or fixing a problem | Stable to growing | People feel less heard once a reply is labeled AI |
| Review or referral ask | Stable to growing | A favor depends on visible personal effort |
| Past-client keep-in-touch | Growing | The most relational message, sent across years between transactions |

(INFERRED: the demand-side research note's (note 07) synthesis. No message type has been tested directly in local services.)

### Where Jump in the Mix stands: aligned in stance, behind in intelligence, invisible to agents

Taken together, the product's stance (the owner reviews and sends every message from their own number, and nothing reads replies) is where the evidence points for relationship follow-up and where the large platforms are converging. That stance is now parity rather than distinction. The product lacks the "who is due next, and why" intelligence its own founder finds missing and that competitors increasingly bundle; G2's 2025 report was headlined "AI Now Means 'Always Included'" [headline-level; page returned 403] ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250514382531/en/G2-Report-AI-Now-Means-Always-Included-Disrupting-All-Stages-of-the-B2B-Software-Buying-Journey)). As a web app with no connector, it is invisible to the assistants owners are starting to use. And with two users and no measurement, it has no distribution engine at all (INFERRED).

## Six verdicts for Kevin

### 1. Survival: the niche survives, but the company has not yet earned it

**Verdict: Jump in the Mix can survive, but nothing yet shows that it will.** The category it occupies, planned relationship follow-up sent by the owner, survives in three of the four futures above and shrinks only if phone and browser assistants absorb the planning as well as the wording, which no platform has announced. The tailwinds are real. Multi-turn reliability lags raw capability; regulation and carrier rules keep autonomous calling and texting expensive; customers penalize suspected AI authorship in exactly the messages the product prepares; and the incumbents' shift to approval boards validates the stance. The company-level risk is different and nearer. With **2 users, 1 workspace and 4 contacts**, no admitted tester, no research and no measurement (VERIFIED-DOC), there is no evidence yet that owners will set it up, open Today and record outcomes, and relationship apps that depend on manual entry tend to fall out of users' routines after the second week ([Dex](https://getdex.com/blog/personal-crm-in-2020-20-startups-apps-and-failed-attempts/), competitor-published). The near-zero cost base (Render's free plan by owner decision, an email budget sized to a free tier, no paid AI or SMS) means the product can stay alive cheaply (INFERRED from `docs/standards/14-release-and-environments.md:144` and `src/lib/env.ts:59-60`). The binding constraint is the founder's time: outstanding owner steps include the iPhone Shortcut, the Google and Apple sign-in apps, restored Actions billing or an uptime monitor, a named inbox for verification, and the tester admission dates (VERIFIED-DOC). Bootstrapped comparables survived by compounding slowly. Less Annoying CRM had about 22,000 users and $2.6M of ARR in 2019, growing about 20% a year through word of mouth and support ([Startup to Last interview with Less Annoying CRM's co-founder](https://www.startuptolast.com/episodes/interview-with-tyler-king-co-founder-and-ceo-of-less-annoying-crm)). Follow Up Boss stayed self-funded for more than a dozen years before Zillow bought it for $400M plus up to $100M in earnout [search excerpt] ([Follow Up Boss blog](https://www.followupboss.com/blog/follow-up-boss-acquired-by-zillow-group)). Compounding needs a working activation and retention loop first, so the honest deadline is roughly two quarters (a proposed checkpoint, not a recorded decision). If the first 10–20 owners activate and keep using Today, the niche is defensible. If they don't, no AI feature will rescue it.

### 2. Use: owners will keep needing a next step sent in their own voice

**Verdict: yes for the relationship layer, no for first-contact speed.** The jobs that persist are deciding who is due and sending a message the customer recognizes as the owner's: estimate chases, post-job check-ins, review and referral asks, seasonal and anniversary check-ins, and keeping in touch with past clients. Those are where the biggest measured loss (silence) meets the strongest preference for a human voice. The first response to a new lead is going the other way: inbound AI answering is cheap and spreading ($29 for 30 conversations at Jobber), consumer agents now phone businesses for quotes, and homeowners increasingly accept AI for first inquiries. Use will depend on effort more than on features. The product already has the right levers: a Today queue, an opt-in morning email, push notifications, Quick Add, phone-contact import, and a first run that recommends one person and one follow-up (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/standards/09-information-architecture-and-user-workflows.md:112`). None has been proven in the field, the daily trigger on iPhone needs a Home Screen install ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)), and Kevin's "stuck in limbo" observation describes the exact failure that ends use: a contact with no next step never comes back to Today (VERIFIED-DOC, PR #124 branch, `docs/MIX_PLACEMENT_AND_STAGES_2026-10-05.md:13`). Use also depends on owners adding one personal detail rather than sending ready-made wording untouched, because identical or generic texts are what recipients recognize as automated and what carriers flag as repetitive (INFERRED from the authorship studies and [CTIA's](https://api.ctia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/230523-CTIA-Messaging-Principles-and-Best-Practices-FINAL.pdf) repetition thresholds).

### 3. Positioning: own number, own voice, a next step for every contact

**Verdict: keep the public category "client follow-up app" and position on who speaks for the business, which is the owner, from their own phone and number, with a next step for every contact. Do not position on "AI" or on "no AI".** Human review no longer differentiates on its own, because Jobber Teammate, Follow Up Boss, Zillow Pro and Superhuman all keep a person approving sends ([Get Alfred on Superhuman](https://get-alfred.ai/blog/best-ai-assistant-for-email-follow-ups), competitor-published). What remains distinct is a combination no competitor reviewed offers together: sending through the phone's own apps from the owner's own number; independence from any field-service suite or brokerage; a low, stable, published price; long-cycle Mixes that keep past clients warm; and plans that can be shared and reused. A "no AI" promise would be fragile. It becomes false the day any AI help is added, and it forecloses useful features. "AI writes your messages" invites the authorship penalty and the FTC's scrutiny of performance and replace-a-human claims ([FTC, Operation AI Comply](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes); [FTC, Air AI](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-sues-stop-air-ai-using-deceptive-claims-about-business-growth-earnings-potential-refund)). "You approve every message, and it goes from your phone" is factual, checkable, and survives any later AI help.

Recorded rules shape how this is said. The category must stay "client follow-up app" until Kevin changes it, and public copy should not call the product a CRM or "phone-first" (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/standards/02-icp-and-positioning.md:86,95`). Copy "MUST NOT claim superiority over other tools", so contrasts must be factual ("your customer hears from you, not a bot") rather than comparative (`02-icp-and-positioning.md:109`). Whether to keep "independent professionals" or name owner-operators, trades and solo real estate is an open question for Kevin (`02-icp-and-positioning.md:181`). The evidence favors naming the segment, because the trades and realtor jobs differ and the profession pages already speak to each. Two homepage entries conflict with the evidence or the record and need Kevin's decision. **Financial advisors** are bound by SEC and FINRA rules that require business texts to be preserved; FINRA fined one firm $750,000 in January 2026 over more than 3,560 unpreserved business texts sent from personal devices ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/finra-fines-benjamin-edwards-750k-over-text-message-failures/265112)), which collides with a send-from-your-own-phone model that keeps no archive. **Sales reps** sits awkwardly with pending decision D37, which would support representatives inside larger companies "without marketing to them" (VERIFIED-DOC, PR #124 branch, `docs/standards-audit-2026-09-28/OWNER_DECISIONS.md:272-335`).

### 4. Local phone AI: the need moves from wording to planning and record

**Verdict: yes, there will still be a need once AI runs on phones, but for a different reason than the homepage gives today.** Phone AI will do the wording (drafts in the owner's style, call summaries, one person's recalled context) for free or nearly free on recent iPhones, Pixels and Galaxies. By 2028 it is unlikely to do the planning: deciding which quiet or past client is due for which step, holding multi-step sequences, recording outcomes across weeks, and keeping a record that survives a new phone or a household that mixes iPhones and Androids. Platform design points the same way. Features react to open threads, are built around one person's account (Magic Cue is blocked in work profiles), are gated by device and language (Siri AI needs an iPhone 15 Pro or later and English at launch), and lean on the cloud rather than being truly local. Third-party sending stays user-confirmed on both platforms, so the handoff Jump in the Mix already uses will not become a disadvantage. The product should therefore stop treating message wording as its core and treat the plan and the record as the core. There is also a benefit that needs no rule change: the handoff lands in the phone's own Messages or Mail app, where the owner's own Writing Tools, Smart Reply or Help Me Write can already polish the draft before the owner sends it (INFERRED from the platform findings). Saying so publicly would be an AI-related claim that needs Kevin's approval under the claims rules (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/standards/02-icp-and-positioning.md:134`, `docs/standards/04-voice-tone-and-copy.md:134`). The risk to watch is Apple or Google shipping a "you haven't been in touch with X" nudge built on personal context. That would cover light, single-person users and leave the owners who need plans, stages and history.

### 5. Marketing: founder-led, with public Mixes and community proof instead of paid ads

**Verdict: market through the founder's presence in owner communities, public, reusable Mixes and earned mentions, and avoid paid acquisition at $15.** The comparable bootstrapped tools grew through word of mouth and support (Less Annoying CRM), through fitting an existing lead flow (Follow Up Boss) and through free peer meetups open even to non-customers (HoneyBook, per search excerpts) ([HoneyBook](https://www.honeybook.com/blog/welcome-to-the-rising-tide-society)). Paid social fits poorly. Facebook lead campaigns cost $13.74 per consumer lead in real estate and $42.95 in home improvement in 2026 benchmarks ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/)), while a back-of-envelope lifetime value for a $15 customer is about $250–$420 and a free sign-up is worth roughly $14–$23 at typical freemium conversion (INFERRED from [ChartMogul's 2023 benchmarks](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-benchmarks-report/), with 64.7% top-quartile annual retention below $25 of monthly revenue per account, and [ChartMogul's 2026 conversion report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/), with 5.5% freemium free-to-paid conversion). Search is changing too. Users clicked a traditional result on 8% of Google visits that showed an AI summary, against 15% without one [independent] ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)); position-one click-through fell 58% when an AI Overview appeared ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)); AI visibility tracks branded web mentions (correlation 0.664) more than links ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)), with Reddit, YouTube and review sites among the most-cited sources ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns)); and an llms.txt file showed no measurable effect ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/llms-txt/)).

The mechanism that fits has four parts. The founder answers follow-up questions where owners already gather (trade and agent Facebook groups, relevant subreddits, local business groups) and gives away working Mixes. Each ready-made or community Mix becomes a public, dated, crawlable page for one situation, such as "estimate with no reply, roofing" or "one-year home anniversary, past client", with a "use this Mix" button that opens an account with the Mix loaded. Short videos show a real follow-up going from Today into the owner's own Messages app. Review-site profiles follow once real users exist. The Marketplace and the versioned file format already exist but hold no listings (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/MARKETPLACE_AND_NUMBERING_2026-09-24.md:47-57`, `docs/FULL_AUDIT_2026-09-24.md:8`), and public Mixes need moderation for Fair Housing wording and the FTC reviews rule ([42 U.S.C. 3604](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). For realtors, the supported message is being the agent past clients call again and send their friends to, using dated, attributed NAR figures without implying that the product produces referrals. For trades, "follow up on every estimate, check in after the job, ask for the review while it's fresh, from you and not a bot" is a hypothesis to test in interviews, because no owner-language research exists (VERIFIED-DOC, `02-icp-and-positioning.md:143`).

Three common growth moves collide with recorded rules and are Kevin's to decide. An owner-to-owner referral credit conflicts with the retired "signup-reward referrals", which "MUST NOT compile into the app" (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/standards/01-product-concept-and-goals.md:115`). Comparison pages against named incumbents must stay factual under the no-superiority rule (`02-icp-and-positioning.md:109`). And measuring the funnel touches the published statement that the app sends no usage events to an analytics provider; counting the steps from confirmed waitlist to first completed follow-up in the product's own database would avoid a third-party collector (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/CONVERSION_COPY.md:23`).

### 6. Direction: keep the trust core, and pivot from template library to next-step engine

**Verdict: do not pivot the core; pivot what the product is for, how wide it is, and how it connects.** The evidence supports everything the constitution protects: the owner sends every message, nothing reads replies, texts come from the owner's own number, and private notes stay out of messages. Those should not move. Five things should.

The first is the value center, which should move from "ready-made Mixes and reminders" to "every contact always has a next step". D33–D35 (a "No Mix" or "No Mix needed" label, a setting for where new contacts go, a last Beat that asks what's next, and Mixes that set stages) answer Kevin's own complaint with deterministic rules at no cost (VERIFIED-DOC, PR #124 branch, `docs/standards-audit-2026-09-28/OWNER_DECISIONS.md:272-316`). They should ship before any AI. The second is breadth, which should give way to depth. A six-role staff console, Marketplace moderation, a Docker edition, a calendar and lead intake serve one workspace today; freezing new surfaces until activation data exists keeps the founder's time on the loop that decides survival (INFERRED). The third is the marketed audience, which should narrow to one or two segments. The evidence leans toward trades owner-operators as the first wedge: the largest pool, the lowest AI adoption (8.9% paid AI use in construction) and the weakest substitutes for relationship follow-up. Sphere-based solo agents come second; their relationship economics are strongest, but free brokerage tools, Cloze and Zillow Pro compete hardest there. D2 already favors trades and solo real estate, and choosing the order is Kevin's decision (VERIFIED-DOC, `02-icp-and-positioning.md:49-51`).

The fourth is connection: the product should become callable by the owner's own assistant. A remote MCP connector with read-only tools (today's due follow-ups, a contact's timeline), draft creation and outcome logging, and no send action, would turn ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini Spark from substitutes into front ends for Jump in the Mix's record. Jobber took this route in September 2026, and Inside Real Estate opened early access to a BackOffice MCP that lets Claude and ChatGPT reach brokerage transaction data ([Jobber MCP](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jobber-launches-mcp-bringing-home-and-commercial-service-data-and-workflows-directly-into-chatgpt-and-claude-302890385.html); [RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/11/inside-real-estate-advances-ai-strategy-launch-complianceai-direct-ai-assistant-integration/), search summary). Anthropic's directory is self-serve for anyone on a paid Claude plan, with review and usage analytics ([Claude docs](https://claude.com/docs/directory/publish)), although no evidence shows a small SaaS acquiring users through any assistant directory. The connector fits the recorded architecture of typed actions the server authorizes and user confirmation for AI-driven multi-record changes (VERIFIED-DOC, `docs/VOICE_PRD_REVIEW.md:19`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:61`). Whether it counts as an AI feature under the standards, and whether to mention it publicly, are Kevin's decisions.

The fifth is AI inside the product, which should become optional by decision rather than absent by rule. The useful AI is not a Mix generator, which aims at the wording phones now supply. It is suggestions the owner approves: turning rough notes into a draft without inventing prices, availability or promises, and proposing the next Mix or stage from facts the owner entered. Building it would require Kevin to amend, through the standards change workflow, the rules that bar the wizard's return and paid AI services (VERIFIED-DOC, `01-product-concept-and-goals.md:115,193`, `11-engineering-architecture.md:208`, `00-constitution.md:62`) and the ban on public AI claims (`02-icp-and-positioning.md:134`), and to set a cost ceiling. It should never send.

Four things should stay off the roadmap. Autonomous sending should stay off: the constitution makes it an explicit, off-by-default option, it is off in production during the beta (`00-constitution.md:39`), and the evidence gives no reason to change that. An inbound AI receptionist is commoditized and outside the product's job. Reading inboxes or detecting replies is barred (`01-product-concept-and-goals.md:54`). A native app should wait for the signposts below (`01-product-concept-and-goals.md:64`). A Google Play listing that wraps the existing web app is a cheap, reversible test of store presence (search excerpt) ([MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/publishing-pwa-app-store/)), but it touches the native-app deferral and carries a one-time developer fee, so it is Kevin's call. An iOS App Store build makes sense only when it adds real native value, because Apple rejects apps that are little more than a "repackaged website" ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

## Conclusion: the retired AI aimed at the part that is becoming free

The July Mix generator automated the wording of follow-up, which phones and assistants now hand owners for free. Kevin's 2026-10-05 complaint points at the part no platform has shipped: knowing, for every contact, what happens next. That reframes the AI question. The product does not need a model to write messages; it needs a reliable next-step engine first, and perhaps later a model that proposes next steps for the owner to approve. It also reorders the risks. The nearest threat is having no users. Next comes assistant absorption of light, single-person use, then incumbents' approval boards inside suites owners already pay for. On-device AI is the most discussed threat and the least dangerous, because platform rules keep third-party sending in the person's hands and platform design keeps memory personal. The cheapest hedge against absorption is to become the record that assistants call; the only test of the nearest threat is ten owners using Today for a month.
