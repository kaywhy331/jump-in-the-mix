# Jump in the Mix design guidelines

The supplied [design export](jump-in-the-mix-designs/index.html) is the visual authority for the homepage and webapp. Preserve its original HTML, tokens and canvas files. Its fixed-size screens illustrate layouts, not production content or product capabilities. When these derived guidelines disagree with the export, the export governs visual composition. Implementation assumptions must be labeled, and automated checks alone do not establish visual fidelity. Use the existing real logo. Consult the [homepage roadmap](../HOMEPAGE_EXPERIENCE_ROADMAP_2026-09-10.md), asset index and relevant fictional ICP hypotheses for content decisions.

## Shared foundation

- `src/styles/design-tokens.css` implements the supplied palette. Use semantic tokens rather than new page-specific hex colors. `design-system.css`, `design-workspaces.css` and `design-public.css` implement common controls and layouts; they load after legacy styles during migration.
- Instrument Sans is the interface font. Newsreader is for a person's words: drafts, message previews, notes and Quick Add capture. Both fonts are hosted locally with OFL licenses in `src/app/fonts`.
- Page, surface, raised and selected are distinct surfaces. Violet button fills remain `--violet` in both themes; readable violet text uses `--violet-text`, marks use `--violet-mark`, and quiet selection uses `--violet-wash`.
- Controls have 12px corners, sheets 24px, conversation bubbles 20px. A bubble tail uses the same fill variable as its body. Use flat rows and fine separators; shadows belong primarily to overlays.
- Give icons explicit dimensions. A clean overflow/contrast scan will not detect an SVG growing to fill a channel tab; inspect control size and the position of the actions in screenshots.
- Every screen supports System, Light and Dark. System follows the operating system. Explicit choices are stored in this browser, synchronize between its tabs and apply before rendering with the request CSP nonce. There is no account-level theme setting. If storage is blocked, the choice applies to the current visit with an explanation.

## Layout and interaction

- Desktop uses a quiet 248px left rail: Quick Add, Today, Contacts, Mixes, Journey and Calendar, then account/settings/help. Landscape tablets and smaller laptops (901–1279px) use an 80px compact rail. Phones and portrait tablets retain a reachable bottom navigation with the central Quick Add bubble and More for secondary destinations.
- Today keeps the queue beside one review pane from 768px, including the supplied 834px portrait tablet; very wide screens add contact context beside the composer. Smaller screens use the review sheet. Opening another review or resizing retains the editor and its draft. Group the queue into Overdue, Today, Later this week (Upcoming in broader date views), and Done. Keep available channel choices, Copy, outcome recording, snooze and skip visible. Unavailable methods stay disabled with their reason. Completion confirmations stack above the bottom navigation and retain Undo. Browser handoff to texting/email is not proof of delivery; retain the existing review, outcome and undo flow.
- Contacts use compact circular-avatar rows with names, stage and next due date, visible totals and last-name sorting. From 1200px, selecting a row keeps the directory beside its profile; a separate details column appears when space permits. On phones the row opens the full profile. The profile shows contact methods, next follow-up, real scheduled dates, current journey stage and history. Notes, preferences and plan management remain reachable. Keep selection, search, import, archive and restore reachable.
- Mixes use a desktop library rail, selected inline editor, day/appointment timing line and a sidebar with real membership and journey usage. Name/start/audience settings collapse for an existing plan. Editing still uses explicit review/save and the existing activation safeguards. Smaller screens use My mixes/Ready-made tabs and compact beat rhythms; their full editor remains available. A Mix is a reusable follow-up plan; preserve manual, date-based, appointment and broadcast behavior.
- Journey uses stage circles sized by actual contact counts. Ring portions classify the earliest pending follow-up as late, due within seven calendar days, or later/unscheduled. Arrows summarize recorded moves over the past 30 days. Smaller touchpoint nodes represent independent existing Mixes; all touchpoints remain reachable in the list. They do not introduce new automation.
- Selecting a stage exposes seven exclusive timing buckets and a roster: Late, Today, Tomorrow, This week, Next week, Later, Nothing scheduled. **Working calendar assumption:** Monday starts a week; Today and Tomorrow take precedence over week groups, even on Sundays. The stage ring separately retains its rolling seven-day window. A dot represents one person on the current page; labels show full filtered counts. Dots open keyboard-accessible person previews with Review, Move, Touchpoint and Open. Select them selects only the unscheduled people on that page; the next step reviews a one-time follow-up in the existing selected-contact workflow. The overview initially selects the first active stage for its roster, keeps the full map, and offers the whole-journey view. Phones show five roster rows with Show all; tablets put the roster before the touchpoint list. The roster is ordered globally by next follow-up, then name/id, with unscheduled people last, at 50 people per page. Dates use the viewer's saved timezone and calendar-day boundaries, including daylight-saving changes. Avoid decorative dots, fabricated movements or sample business data.
- Calendar presents a compact dated agenda alongside the selected event editor and, on wide desktops, contact context. Date/search filters and event management actions are disclosures. On phones the agenda leads; New event and Edit reveal the form. The initial desktop selection is the first editable local event in the displayed range, while New event and a supplied contact explicitly start a new form. Follow-ups link to Today for review. Meetings and time blocks retain their existing scheduling, conflict, import and connection behavior.
- **Demo contract (owner clarification, September 19):** The homepage and every profession situation simulate the Today review workflow with one explicitly fictional contact and exactly three Beats: SMS today (day 0), email tomorrow (day 1), and a phone call in one week (day 7). Show the whole schedule, editable channel-specific drafts, previews, and user-recorded outcomes with Undo. Future previews retain their due labels. All practice remains in memory; no native handoff, actual send, call, or account scheduling occurs. This supersedes the export’s edit/preview/record progress labels for the demo only; preserve its visual language and the original export. Real starter Mix timing remains separate.
- Homepage and all five “Mixes for” profession pages use the shared `PublicLanding` composition: hero/demo, how it works, own-voice explanation, profession directory, and waitlist. Keep profession-specific wording, related templates, demo situations and access context. The “Mixes for” navigation has five profession links, with no “All” chip; the logo returns home. Keep the five links on one row when desktop space permits and wrap them naturally on narrower screens.
- Homepage uses open full-width bands, strong headings, the conversational demo, a simple three-step rhythm and a contrasting waitlist section. Preserve verified access and product claims. No invented testimonials, pricing or automatic-send promises.
- Apply the same foundation to account, settings, onboarding, authentication, support, staff and empty/error states. Mockups without matching product behavior require a product decision, not a misleading control.
- For pages without a mockup, choose a shared structure: unmocked workspaces and data lists use a 1440px maximum; the supplied multi-column workspaces can use 1672px beside the 248px rail at 1920px; account/help/conversation pages use `page--content` (1120px); focused contact, business and preference forms use `page--form` (1040px). Settings and More use the same flat destination rows within 960px. These are maximum widths; every layout contracts to the available space. Keep headers, actions, semantic colors, controls and navigation consistent across these structures.
- Size columns against their own bounded workspace. Today reserves a 300–440px queue and a 24px gap, then gives the remaining space to review. Never simulate a column with percentage padding against an uncapped parent. Long prose and forms should remain readable instead of stretching across an entire ultrawide monitor.

## Verification for future changes

Contacts implementation contract: the Next card selects the earliest pending follow-up and shows its own Mix occurrence, including recorded completed Beats. Appointment bindings and saved occurrence dates identify that sequence; unrelated recurring runs must never be joined. Older records without enough occurrence data show an unavailable date instead of an invented schedule. The personal Journey shows recorded changes (including returns to earlier stages), then clearly undated possible next stages. “Start a touchpoint” offers the existing Mix assignment and one-time follow-up workflows; it does not create inactivity automation. Persistent contact notes stay in Notes to remember, while dated updates appear in History. Directory sorting and filtering retain the selected person while they still match the filters. Secondary tools remain accessible through More and the profile's management disclosure.

Check light and dark themes, explicit overrides and system mode. Exercise narrow phones (320/390), portrait tablets (834), landscape/laptop sizes (1194/1440), wide desktop (1920), and ultrawide/4K (2560/3440/3840) when changing layout. Include widths immediately around navigation and column breakpoints (767/768, 900/901, 1099/1100, 1199/1200, 1279/1280). Check keyboard focus, readable contrast, usable touch targets, long names, empty states, dense real-data fixtures and horizontal overflow. Measure minimum usable column widths and check for overlapping content; an overflow-only scan missed the original ultrawide queue collapse. Inspect screenshots as well as automated results. Run the relevant workflow checks, typecheck and build before claiming completion.

## Product decisions still open

1. Whether appearance should synchronize across devices with an account. Current implementation is browser-local, matching the export's data-theme mechanism.
2. Whether touchpoints eventually become a distinct feature. Current implementation uses independent Mixes and existing assignments/pending follow-ups, including group assignments.
3. Quick Add’s multi-item Save all, existing-person matching, event interpretation and atomicity still need a product contract. The current correction retains the note, allows editing recognized person/follow-up details, and continues to the established form. It does not claim to implement the richer save workflow.
4. The mockup's “after 60 quiet days” needs an inactivity rule and a definition of what resets that clock (completed follow-up or any recorded interaction). No inactivity automation has been inferred or enabled.

These are implementation assumptions pending the owner's answers, not measured customer outcomes or promises of future features.

The branded Quick Add navigation bubble is the explicit exception to BubbleKit’s “navigation never gets a tail”; ordinary navigation links have no tails.
