## Shared foundation

- `src/styles/design-tokens.css` implements the supplied palette. Use semantic tokens rather than new page-specific hex colors. `design-system.css`, `design-workspaces.css` and `design-public.css` implement common controls and layouts; they load after legacy styles during migration.
- Instrument Sans is the interface font. Newsreader is for a person's words: drafts, message previews, notes and Quick Add capture. Both fonts are hosted locally with OFL licenses in `src/app/fonts`.
- Page, surface, raised and selected are distinct surfaces. Violet button fills remain `--violet` in both themes; readable violet text uses `--violet-text`, marks use `--violet-mark`, and quiet selection uses `--violet-wash`.
- Controls have 12px corners, sheets 24px, conversation bubbles 20px. A bubble tail uses the same fill variable as its body. Use flat rows and fine separators; shadows belong primarily to overlays.
- Give icons explicit dimensions. A clean overflow/contrast scan will not detect an SVG growing to fill a channel tab; inspect control size and the position of the actions in screenshots.
- Every screen supports System, Light and Dark. System follows the operating system. Explicit choices are stored in this browser, synchronize between its tabs and apply before rendering with the request CSP nonce. Synchronization belongs in the root layout so sign-in, recovery and other screens without a picker also update. There is no account-level theme setting. If storage is blocked, the choice applies to the current visit with an explanation.

## Layout and interaction

- Desktop uses a quiet 248px left rail: Quick Add, Today, Contacts, Mixes, Journey and Calendar, then account/settings/help. Landscape tablets and smaller laptops (901–1279px) use an 80px compact rail. Phones and portrait tablets retain a reachable bottom navigation with the central Quick Add bubble and More for secondary destinations.
- Both desktop rails scroll independently when the window is short. Keep their controls at usable sizes, reveal keyboard focus, and retain access to Appearance and Profile below the primary destinations. Include a 600px-high laptop check as well as phone and tablet widths.
- Today keeps the queue beside one review pane from 768px, including the supplied 834px portrait tablet; very wide screens add contact context beside the composer. Smaller screens use the review sheet. Opening another review or resizing retains the editor and its draft. Group the queue into Overdue, Today, Later this week (Upcoming in broader date views), and Done. Keep available channel choices, Copy, outcome recording, snooze and skip visible. Unavailable methods stay disabled with their reason. Completion confirmations stack above the bottom navigation and retain Undo. Browser handoff to texting/email is not proof of delivery; retain the existing review, outcome and undo flow.
- The Today review's pending-Beat preview is a horizontal track belonging to the selected Mix occurrence, using the same occurrence matching as Contacts. Keep the selected Beat visible even outside the six-item contact preview. This is a bounded pending preview, not a full completed/scheduled sequence. Undated legacy occurrences must not be joined speculatively. A compose field stays transparent inside its bubble in both phone sheets and docked review; verify its computed background as well as the surrounding fill.
- Contacts use compact circular-avatar rows with names, stage and next due date, visible totals and last-name sorting. From 1200px, selecting a row keeps the directory beside its profile; a separate details column appears when space permits. On phones the row opens the full profile. The profile shows contact methods, next follow-up, real scheduled dates, current journey stage and history. Notes, preferences and plan management remain reachable. Keep selection, search, import, archive and restore reachable.
- Mixes use a desktop library rail, selected inline editor, day/appointment timing line and a sidebar with real membership and journey usage. Name/start/audience settings collapse for an existing plan. Editing uses explicit save. Personal preferences choose review before activation (default) or direct activation on save; direct mode also offers Review first. Apply that preference to the editor, ready-made setup and list activation, and enforce it on the server. Smaller screens use My mixes/Ready-made tabs and compact beat rhythms; their full editor remains available. A Mix is a reusable follow-up plan; preserve manual, date-based, appointment and broadcast behavior.
- Journey uses stage circles sized by actual contact counts. Ring portions classify the earliest pending follow-up as late, due within seven calendar days, or later/unscheduled. Arrows summarize recorded moves over the past 30 days. Smaller touchpoint nodes represent independent existing Mixes and the opt-in “Just saying hello” quiet-day suggestions. All touchpoints remain reachable in the list. The quiet-day node sits below the stages and shows actual due people; it schedules no messages and does not change stages.
- Movement arrows end outside the stage rings and selection halos. Use straight links for recorded moves to the next stage, upper curves for skips, and lower curves for returns; provide the same source, destination and count to assistive technology. Touchpoint captions belong above the upper row or below the lower row, away from their connecting lines. Reserve lower space only for actual lower nodes or return arrows.
- The Journey desktop workspace uses the same 1672px maximum as the other supplied multi-column workspaces. Its map shows up to six touchpoints, always including the selected touchpoint and the enabled quiet-day node; the complete list stays available. Space upper and lower rows independently and center single nodes. Quiet-day person names use Instrument Sans, like other directory rows; Newsreader remains for personal wording.
- Journey does not reserve an empty second row of touchpoint nodes. On phones, stages and people lead; the Journey options sheet retains stage/transition settings, the whole-journey view and All contacts.
- Portrait-tablet Journey keeps the compact page header and search/options sheets through 900px. Avoid stacking a global header, page header, settings row and view links above the map; the people roster should remain visible below it. Landscape layouts restore the rail and desktop controls.
- Selecting a stage exposes seven exclusive timing buckets and a roster: Late, Today, Tomorrow, This week, Next week, Later, Nothing scheduled. **Working calendar assumption:** Monday starts a week; Today and Tomorrow take precedence over week groups, even on Sundays. The stage ring separately retains its rolling seven-day window. A dot represents one person on the current page; labels show full filtered counts. Dots open keyboard-accessible person previews with Review, Move, Touchpoint and Open. Select them selects only the unscheduled people on that page; the next step reviews a one-time follow-up in the existing selected-contact workflow. The overview initially selects the first active stage for its roster, keeps the full map, and offers the whole-journey view. Phones show five roster rows with Show all; tablets put the roster before the touchpoint list. The roster is ordered globally by next follow-up, then name/id, with unscheduled people last, at 50 people per page. Dates use the viewer's saved timezone and calendar-day boundaries, including daylight-saving changes. Avoid decorative dots, fabricated movements or sample business data.
- Calendar presents a compact dated agenda alongside the selected event editor and, on wide desktops, contact context. Date/search filters and event management actions are disclosures. On phones the agenda leads; New event and Edit reveal the form. The initial desktop selection is the first editable local event in the displayed range, while New event and a supplied contact explicitly start a new form. Follow-ups link to Today for review. Meetings and time blocks retain their existing scheduling, conflict, import and connection behavior.
- **Demo contract (owner clarification, September 19):** The homepage and every profession situation simulate the Today review workflow with one explicitly fictional contact and exactly three Beats: SMS today (day 0), email tomorrow (day 1), and a phone call in one week (day 7). Show the whole schedule, editable channel-specific drafts, previews, and user-recorded outcomes with Undo. Future previews retain their due labels. All practice remains in memory; no native handoff, actual send, call, or account scheduling occurs. This supersedes the export’s edit/preview/record progress labels for the demo only; preserve its visual language and the original export. Real starter Mix timing remains separate.
- Homepage and all five “Mixes for” profession pages use the shared `PublicLanding` composition: hero/demo, how it works, own-voice explanation, profession directory, and waitlist. Keep profession-specific wording, related templates, demo situations and access context. The “Mixes for” navigation has five profession links, with no “All” chip; the logo returns home. Keep the five links on one row when desktop space permits and wrap them naturally on narrower screens.
- Homepage uses open full-width bands, strong headings, the conversational demo, a simple three-step rhythm and a contrasting waitlist section. Preserve verified access and product claims. No invented testimonials, pricing or automatic-send promises.
- Apply the same foundation to account, settings, onboarding, authentication, support, staff and empty/error states. Mockups without matching product behavior require a product decision, not a misleading control.
- More uses compact grouped destination rows with a minimum 44px target, lighter labels and a reachable Appearance picker and Sign out. Account/session/support lists use flat rows and separators; support message bodies use Newsreader, with neutral incoming and violet outgoing surfaces. Status colors describe status rather than a speaker's identity. Desktop account/help navigation identifies its current destination, including nested pages.
- For pages without a mockup, choose a shared structure: unmocked workspaces and data lists use a 1440px maximum; the supplied multi-column workspaces can use 1672px beside the 248px rail at 1920px; account/help/conversation pages use `page--content` (1120px); focused contact, business and preference forms use `page--form` (1040px). Settings and More use the same flat destination rows within 960px. These are maximum widths; every layout contracts to the available space. Keep headers, actions, semantic colors, controls and navigation consistent across these structures.
- Size columns against their own bounded workspace. Today reserves a 300–440px queue and a 24px gap, then gives the remaining space to review. Never simulate a column with percentage padding against an uncapped parent. Long prose and forms should remain readable instead of stretching across an entire ultrawide monitor.

## Pane and control details

- Today keeps person identity above Mix/timing, then wording, channel choice and send/outcome controls. The message label remains accessible without consuming a separate visual row. Copy belongs beside the handoff; save status and discard belong below it. Errors, missing methods, automatic-delivery holds and draft conflicts remain visible. On phones, check that the ordinary short-message review exposes the handoff and outcomes together; long content can scroll.
- Mix beats show their actual editable name, day/minute offset, channel and wording together. Beat options disclose appointment timing, alternate versions, reordering and removal. Disclosed fields stay mounted so saving retains their values. Store the entered Beat name exactly; do not regenerate a channel label or repeatedly prefix it with the Mix name. Library tracks show actual Beat counts and explicit timing summaries, not invented activity.
- Calendar separates its directory, editor and contact context. Put title/type first, then date/duration, contact, timezone and place. Extra meeting details and event management actions are disclosures. Keep the save/share controls beside the no-invitation explanation. The agenda uses the viewer's timezone; the editor identifies the event's timezone and preserves the existing clock-time behavior.
- At desktop widths with enough review space (1200px and above), Today puts Snooze and Skip beside the person heading, with Mark sent beside the send controls. Reserve space for long names and the context pane; on phones and portrait tablets keep the outcomes below the wording.
- Saved contact views live in Filter, including empty and small result sets. Saving a view retains the chosen directory sort. Contact tools use readable single-column destination rows. Auto-saving preferences must not leave invisible submit buttons in the keyboard path.
- Scope a docked pane’s panel/header/body styles to its direct children so nested dialogs retain their own dimensions, surface and controls. At short viewport heights, allow editor headers and action footers to scroll instead of covering fields. Timezone selection identifies the current zone first, exposes selection without relying on color and calls browser detection “Use device timezone.”
- Popups use one clear heading, readable supporting copy and a 44px close control. Bound long dialogs to the viewport and retain scrolling. Confirmation buttons place Cancel before the consequential action; initial focus stays on Cancel. Journey previews must measure their content and fit above or below the triggering dot instead of assuming a fixed height.
- Popup titles are 18px/700 with 14px supporting copy in `--text-2`; phone sheets (≤767px) are bottom sheets with a grabber and borderless header; overlays use the `--scrim` token. Ask for confirmation with `ConfirmDialog` or `useConfirmDialog`, never `window.confirm` (only the synchronous navigation guard stays native). Every disclosure uses the shared pattern in `disclosures.css`: a trailing 16px chevron, a 44px target and a 14px/600 label; per-item actions use the 44px round More control. See [the consistency record](DESIGN_CONSISTENCY_2026-09-23.md).
- Phone Journey search opens from the header; desktop search stays in the roster. Touchpoint headings use ordinary case and a compact add control. Shared More icons must match their destination.
- The phone public header retains the wordmark, a short Join label with its full accessible name, and an Explore menu containing Sign in. Escape, outside clicks and link selection close that menu. Phone/tablet hero order is introduction, demo, profession choices; desktop keeps choices beside the demo. The profession directory uses two columns on phones, as in the export; stack its entries when enlarged text or a narrower available width would clip the labels. Footer descriptions must not inherit a vertical flex basis that creates empty space.

- Preserve native dialog stacking when a docked review changes to a modal on resize: an open child picker stays above its parent, Escape closes only the current layer, and focus and drafts survive.
- Reveal invalid fields inside collapsed disclosures before browser validation focuses them. Failed saves and required person choices must receive visible focus and retain all entered values, including on a repeated failure.
- Form grids must shrink around their contents and action rows must wrap. On phone/tablet Calendar and Mix editors, save actions scroll with the form so enlarged copy cannot cover fields. Keyboard focus must stay clear of persistent navigation and save bars.

## Verification for future changes

Contacts implementation contract: the Next card selects the earliest pending follow-up and shows its own Mix occurrence, including recorded completed Beats. Appointment bindings and saved occurrence dates identify that sequence; unrelated recurring runs must never be joined. Older records without enough occurrence data show an unavailable date instead of an invented schedule. The personal Journey shows recorded changes (including returns to earlier stages), then clearly undated possible next stages. “Start a touchpoint” offers the existing Mix assignment and one-time follow-up workflows; it does not create inactivity automation. Persistent contact notes stay in Notes to remember, while dated updates appear in History. Directory sorting and filtering retain the selected person while they still match the filters. Secondary tools remain accessible through More and the profile's management disclosure.

Check light and dark themes, explicit overrides and system mode. Exercise narrow phones (320/390), portrait tablets (834), landscape/laptop sizes (1194/1440), wide desktop (1920), and ultrawide/4K (2560/3440/3840) when changing layout. Include widths immediately around navigation and column breakpoints (767/768, 900/901, 1099/1100, 1199/1200, 1279/1280). Check keyboard focus, readable contrast, usable touch targets, long names, empty states, dense real-data fixtures and horizontal overflow. Include 200% text sizing, hidden-field validation, repeated save failures, and resizing while a child dialog is open; font-size stress is distinct from actual browser zoom. Measure minimum usable column widths and check for overlapping content; an overflow-only scan missed the original ultrawide queue collapse. Inspect screenshots as well as automated results. Run the relevant workflow checks, typecheck and build before claiming completion.

## Product decisions still open

1. Whether appearance should synchronize across devices with an account. Current implementation is browser-local, matching the export's data-theme mechanism.
2. Whether general touchpoints eventually become a distinct feature. Mix touchpoints use independent Mixes and existing assignments/pending follow-ups, including group assignments. Quiet-day suggestions now have the separate lifecycle described below.
3. **Resolved September 20:** the owner approved combined Quick Add. Review editable person, private note, event and follow-up rows, allow explicit existing-person selection, and save selected items atomically with retry protection. Keep times and message wording editable; require unstated times and follow-up methods rather than inventing them. New-person duplicates and calendar overlaps require explicit choices. Quick Add follow-ups stay manual even with automatic sending enabled. See [implementation decisions](QUICK_ADD_AND_ACTIVATION_2026-09-20.md).
4. **Resolved September 20:** a quiet-day suggestion stays actionable until a completed follow-up, a logged conversation, or an explicit Skip/Completed decision resolves it. Completed meetings count as conversations; ordinary notes and message handoffs do not. Each resolution starts a fresh quiet period. The workspace setting is opt-in, starts at 60 calendar days, and accepts 1–365 days. Today shows a short list; Journey’s “Just saying hello” opens the searchable, paginated list. Neither resolving nor suggesting moves a stage or sends a message. Existing people already due can appear on enablement. See [the lifecycle and verification](QUIET_DAY_TOUCHPOINTS_2026-09-20.md).
5. **Resolved September 20:** the owner approved both activation modes, chosen per user in Account → Personal preferences → Starting mixes. Review remains the default; direct mode activates on an explicit save/start. Changing this preference never activates existing drafts by itself.

Unresolved items remain implementation assumptions pending the owner’s answers. Resolved decisions describe implementation, not measured customer outcomes or promises of future features.

The branded Quick Add navigation bubble is the explicit exception to BubbleKit’s “navigation never gets a tail”; ordinary navigation links have no tails.
