# Mobile experience audit — September 6, 2026

The bottom navigation was visibly off-center. That issue is fixed and published to [the dedicated test site](https://jump-in-the-mix-test.netlify.app). The broader review also found a broken tablet contact-row layout, import-screen accessibility issues, inconsistent touch targets, and opportunities to reduce header height and excess bottom spacing. Those findings remain open; this release changes the shared footer only.

Commit `e11ea4a` was built and published first as deployment `6a9e1a93dd6590626cf79f82`. The footer correction was then built and published as `6a9e1caa3ef5547c8e541be6`, using the same commit plus the local CSS patch. Both deployments' 11 rendered CSS/JavaScript assets matched the adapter build before and after promotion. The separate production-site link is preserved.

**Scope and evidence**

The baseline review covered 31 pages and three dialog types, totaling **414 rendered viewport/theme states** and **64 automated accessibility scans**. The authenticated pages were measured at 320×720, 390×844, 430×932, 768×1024, 844×390, 900×900, and 901×900 CSS pixels, in light and dark themes. Public pages were checked at the three phone sizes in light mode. Quick Add, contact filters, and plan actions were also checked at short viewport heights, including 390×430 and landscape 844×390.

This was a text/DOM-based browser review: computed styles, element geometry, clipping, hit-target candidates, keyboard interaction, and axe checks, cross-checked against the source. No screenshots, videos, or traces were captured or viewed. Consequently, the findings establish measurable layout and interaction behavior; they do not represent a visual-art-direction review from screenshots or a physical-device qualification.

Hosted browsing used the existing test account and sample data. After sign-in, application mutation requests were blocked; none were attempted. Import execution, changes to contacts/plans/settings, delivery, provider connections, and account administration were outside this read-only audit.

**Findings, in recommended order**

| Priority / status | Finding and reproduction | Recommended treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High — **fixed** | **Footer has an empty sixth column.** At 390px, five controls occupy five of six 65px columns: Quick Add is centered at 162.5px instead of 195px, and 65px is empty on the right. At 768–900px, the footer also becomes an inset floating bar and Quick Add stretches horizontally. The phone bar's inner row extended 1px past the viewport bottom. | The shared footer now uses five equal columns, meets the left/right/bottom viewport edges through 900px, and keeps Quick Add at 52×52px in the center. A 66px row plus its border and safe-area padding determines the bar height. Remove conflicting footer rules from the support and phone stylesheets so there is one layout definition. |
| High — open | **Tablet contact rows place controls in the wrong grid cells.** Open Contacts at 768px, 844px, or 900px. The normal row has three visible children—selection control, contact link, and actions—but only two grid columns. A measured 720px-wide row resolves to columns `0px 674px`; the actions wrap into a second row with a zero-width container, and the More button is squeezed to 26×48px. The sample row is about 242px tall. | Hide the selection control outside selection mode consistently through the tablet breakpoint, or explicitly allocate all three children. Give the actions a dedicated column and a single full-size trigger. Verify normal and selection modes separately, including pointer hit testing and text fit. |
| High — open | **Import's active step is hard to read in dark mode.** On Contact Import at 320px, the active step label has measured contrast **1.64:1**, with pale text `#c7bfff` against `#fbfaff`. The active background is hard-coded in `contact-import.css`, while its foreground follows the dark-theme token. | Use a matched foreground/background token pair for normal, active, and completed steps. Verify every import state in both themes. Normal-size text generally needs 4.5:1 contrast under [WCAG's contrast criterion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html). |
| Medium — open | **Import progress can scroll horizontally but is not keyboard focusable.** The six-step list becomes a horizontal strip on phones. Axe reports `scrollable-region-focusable` in both themes; the list has no focusable children or tab stop. | Prefer a compact current-step indicator with an accessible full progress list, or give the scroll region keyboard access, an accessible name, and a visible focus indicator. Verify reaching the final step with a keyboard. |
| Medium — open | **Touch sizing is inconsistent.** In an existing date-triggered plan, “Manage date types” measures about 117×18px, with insufficient separation from the adjacent control; axe reports `target-size` in both themes. Contact-form disclosures and advanced plan disclosures are only 24px tall. Business service-reorder buttons are 38×38px. | Make the standalone date-types action large enough and separate it from the field label. Aim for 44px mobile targets for disclosures and reorder actions. Distinguish the [24px AA minimum and its exceptions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html) from the [44px enhanced target](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html): the 24px and 38px measurements alone are not AA failures. |
| Medium — open design recommendation | **Headers consume inconsistent amounts of vertical space.** At 320–390px, Today is about 124px tall, Contacts 150px, and Calendar 155px, compared with 60px for More and the settings hub. At 430px, Today drops to 64px and Contacts to 90px. At landscape 844×390, Today/Calendar headers alone are about 145px. | Establish a consistent title/action row, followed by an intentional search/filter row where needed. Keep essential actions accessible when labels wrap; avoid relying on blanket label hiding. The desired outcome is predictable content starting positions and more room for actual work. These measurements indicate an opportunity, not a claim that all multirow headers are defects. |
| Low — open design recommendation | **Footer clearance is reserved in multiple nested containers.** Phone layouts have 68px bottom padding on the shell, 76px on main, and 24px on the page: 168px before any page-specific spacing. Several layers also add the safe-area inset. | Give one shared shell rule responsibility for the footer reserve, with separate spacing only where sticky actions need it. Verify long contact forms, bulk-selection actions, last-row reachability, and short viewports before reducing the padding. |

**What is working**

Across the baseline states there was no document-level horizontal overflow, every page had one `main` landmark, and no application exceptions or same-origin HTTP errors were recorded. Measured page headers meet the phone viewport edges; the ordinary content gutters are consistently 12px at 320px and 16px at 390–430px. The primary alignment problem is therefore not a universal left margin error: it comes from particular grid definitions, breakpoint changes, and stacked spacing rules.

No measured visible text-entry/select control used a font smaller than 16px. This is a typography observation, not a physical-iOS keyboard/zoom result. Quick Add, contact filters, and plan-action dialogs closed with Escape and returned focus to their trigger in both themes. The baseline's four failing accessibility scans concerned the import screen and the existing plan editor; they represent three distinct rule failures, including the two import rules.

Some controls in long lists were underneath the fixed footer at the initial scroll position. That by itself is expected with a scrolling list and was not counted as a blocked interaction. Bottom-of-page reachability must be checked separately; similarly, intentional ellipsis and horizontally scrollable content must not be confused with document overflow. The contact action's zero-width container was independently inspected and confirmed as a defect.

**Footer change and verification**

The implementation is in [base.css](../src/styles/base.css); obsolete footer overrides were removed from [mobile-ux.css](../src/styles/mobile-ux.css) and [support.css](../src/styles/support.css). The active destination remains indicated, label sizes remain readable, and the desktop sidebar returns above 900px.

The strengthened [navigation regression](../e2e/visual-fitment.spec.ts) fails against the original build on unequal control centers, then passes against the correction. It checks all five controls, full footer width, the bottom edge, minimum control dimensions, exact center spacing, Quick Add opening/focus restoration, and the transition to the desktop sidebar. It covers 320, 390, 430, 767, 768, 844, and 900px widths, plus Chromium safe-area overrides for portrait (34px bottom) and landscape (44px sides, 21px bottom).

The corrected Netlify production build, TypeScript, and six focused desktop/mobile layout tests passed; four project-specific entries were intentionally skipped. The additional safe-area regression passed separately. This CSS change did not rerun the full application unit/integration suite. Test execution used an isolated local PostgreSQL fixture.

After publication, a focused hosted sweep covered **150 viewport/theme states and 24 accessibility scans**, checking nine representative pages plus the three dialog types. Footer edge/center assertions passed, all checked final controls could scroll above the footer, and the dialogs restored focus. This subset produced no axe violations or application exceptions; it does not clear the separately recorded import and existing-plan-editor findings. The tablet contact-row clipping remains visible in this sweep.

The first post-publication sweep stopped after 124 states on an HTTP 403 while loading Settings in dark mode. Its original report is preserved. A continuation completed only the outstanding views, bringing the combined report to 150 states. The 403 is retained in the network record; its cause was not established, and this report does not claim an error-free network run.

Final web/database health checks returned 200, private access still required authentication, and the data guard preserved 13 contacts, five plans, and their original plan fingerprint. Worker health also returned 200 after browsing; this does not establish unattended recurrence. The owned local fixture database was removed, the original local database remained present, and the test application listener stopped. The CSS, regression test, and this report remain uncommitted in the checkout; no Git push was performed.

Raw browser measurements, deployment receipts, logs, and private configuration are retained in ignored `.artifacts/mobile-review-2026-09-06/`. Physical iPhone/Android use, Safari/Firefox behavior, native keyboards, browser chrome, installed-PWA behavior, screen readers, nonzero safe areas on actual hardware, and subjective visual polish remain separate checks.
