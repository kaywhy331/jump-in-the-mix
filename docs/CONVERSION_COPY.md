# Conversion copy decisions

Updated September 8, 2026. These are message hypotheses supported by the implemented product, not claims of measured conversion improvement.

Joanna Wiebe’s [headline guidance](https://copyhackers.com/2013/09/writing-powerful-headlines/) emphasizes matching visitor expectations, clear language, getting to the reader’s concern, and communicating a useful benefit. Her [message-hierarchy example](https://copyhackers.com/2016/06/copywriting-principles-sweatblock/) gives each message a purpose rather than treating a page as an assortment of sections.

The homepage now leads with “Know who to follow up with. And what to say.” The subhead explains the actual sequence: keep contacts together, choose a time, personalize a prepared message, use the daily list. Business, personal, and network uses remain visible. The interactive demo and explicitly fictional daily list provide observable product evidence.

The primary next step is joining the free-account waitlist. Nearby copy explains email confirmation and access by invitation. The FAQ answers cost, contact import, sending control, data control, and invitation selection. It now accurately distinguishes review-first handoffs, optional connected-provider sending, and direct System Mix invitation email. The closing copy describes the first useful task and what happens after signup.

No customer testimonials, usage statistics, revenue claims, conversion lifts, invented deadlines, guaranteed selection dates, or guarantees of viral growth were added. Weekly selection explains the real rule: up to ten eligible requests every seven days, five oldest and five random. A signup is not an account or an invitation.

Still required before claiming validated marketing effectiveness: collect actual prospect/customer language, observe new-user comprehension and task completion, identify the highest-intent audience, and measure confirmed waitlist requests → issued invitations → verified accounts → completed useful follow-ups. Keep private contact content and access URLs out of those measurements. Use those observations to revise the message rather than inventing customer research.
