## The strongest initial customer for Jump in the Mix

**I would build your first focused marketing campaign around an established, referral-driven solo real estate agent who is excellent with people—but inconsistent at staying in touch.**

Not a brand-new agent with no network. Not a large team looking for enterprise automation. Someone who already has valuable relationships, personally handles their communication, and regularly thinks:

> “I meant to reach out to them. Now it’s been three weeks, and I don’t know how to restart the conversation.”

The broader customer category is **the relationship-driven independent professional**. Real estate is my recommended first segment to test—not a reason to limit the entire product to real estate.

**The profile below is a fictional composite for marketing and product planning, not an actual customer or a validated description of your users.** Personal details, income, routines, contact counts, and lifestyle preferences are illustrative hypotheses. The cited product and industry facts provide the grounding.

---

## Why this person is a strong match

Your public website centers on keeping relationship context together, organizing follow-up into Mixes, preparing editable messages, and working through a daily list. It also emphasizes that people review and send through their own messaging apps by default. That supports **personal follow-through**, rather than simply sending more messages. ([Jump in the Mix][1])

Residential real estate offers a plausible starting market because of its independent working structure. NAR’s June 2026 Member Profile reports that **86% of members are independent contractors**, while 21% work on teams. The report also highlights the role of referrals, repeat clients, and durable relationships. This supports testing a solo-professional segment, although it does not prove demand for Jump in the Mix. ([National Association of REALTORS®][2])

The proposed benefit also aligns with stated technology priorities: NAR’s 2025 Technology Survey found that **66% adopted technology primarily to save time, and 64% to improve the client experience**. My inference: lead with making follow-through easier and more personal—not with a long list of automation features. ([National Association of REALTORS®][3])

### Working style

Maya is **socially proactive but administratively reactive**.

She listens well during a meeting, remembers a client’s concerns, and makes people feel comfortable. The breakdown happens afterward: an appointment runs late, a document needs attention, and the promised check-in never becomes a scheduled next step.

She works in short, interrupted blocks. A tool that requires a lengthy weekly administration session is a poor fit. Something she can use between appointments is much more plausible.

She is not opposed to technology. She is opposed to **maintaining technology**.

Her ideal interaction is:

> “Show me the person, remind me why I’m contacting them, and help me get started. I’ll take it from there.”

**Important qualification:** Maya is willing to personally complete follow-ups. Someone who wants all relationship work to happen without their involvement is a different customer.

## What motivates her—and what makes her disengage

### Her practical and emotional drivers

| Driver                        | What she consciously wants                                         | What it means underneath                                            |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A dependable reputation**   | To follow through on what she says she will do.                    | “People should feel they can count on me.”                          |
| **More consistent business**  | Fewer long gaps between promising conversations and opportunities. | “I want less uncertainty about where future work will come from.”   |
| **Personal communication**    | Messages that sound natural and reflect the relationship.          | “I do not want people to feel they are inside a campaign.”          |
| **A manageable workload**     | A small, clear set of next actions.                                | “I want to know what matters without rebuilding my plan every day.” |
| **Professional independence** | To run her own business without hiring a larger support structure. | “I want to grow without losing control of my time or how I work.”   |
| **Visible progress**          | To see that she followed through, not merely collected contacts.   | “I need evidence that I am doing the work that matters.”            |
| **Family presence**           | Less avoidable catch-up work at night.                             | “Success should improve my life, not consume it.”                   |

### What she likes

She likes thoughtful service, straightforward language, useful defaults, small completed tasks, helpful introductions, and tools that make her feel capable.

She appreciates an assistant that offers a starting point without taking over her voice.

### What she dislikes

She dislikes generic sales scripts, excessive reminders, complicated setup, duplicate data entry, surprise charges, difficult cancellation, and being pushed into a communication style that feels unnatural.

She is especially resistant to products that seem to say, **“First, reorganize your entire business.”**

### What demotivates her

A large overdue backlog can make her avoid opening the tool. A quiet period with few replies can make her question whether the effort is working. An irrelevant suggestion can make the system feel like more noise.

The product response should be practical: a manageable next action, easy rescheduling, relevant context, and a way to restart without cleaning up weeks of history first.

### Her representative inner dialogue

> “I don’t need another place to store people. I need help actually staying in touch.”

> “I know follow-up matters. I just don’t want every message to sound like I’m asking for business.”

> “My biggest concern isn’t the subscription. It’s whether I’ll still use this after the first week.”

## The Jump in the Mix use cases that matter most to her

Your site describes Mixes as sequences of messages and reminders, with individual Beats representing touchpoints. The following are **proposed applications of that model**, not claims that industry-specific templates or integrations are already available. ([Jump in the Mix][1])

| Situation                                              | The workflow to demonstrate                                                      | Why Maya would care                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A prospective client is interested but not ready**   | Record their stated timing and create a relevant future check-in.                | The person does not disappear simply because there is no immediate transaction. |
| **She meets someone at an open house or introduction** | Capture the conversation context and choose an appropriate next step.            | Avoids collecting details without continuing the relationship.                  |
| **A client completes a transaction**                   | Plan useful post-transaction check-ins around the person’s circumstances.        | Helps her remain helpful after the immediate work ends.                         |
| **She receives a professional referral**               | Follow through on the introduction and remember to thank the person who made it. | Supports trust with the people who recommend her.                               |
| **A past client has gone quiet**                       | Prepare a specific, relevant reconnection message.                               | Removes some of the awkwardness of restarting a conversation.                   |
| **She learns about an important milestone**            | Record a reminder and decide what kind of acknowledgment is appropriate.         | Makes attentiveness easier to sustain.                                          |
| **A friend keeps slipping off her radar**              | Keep a personal check-in separate from business follow-up.                       | Extends the value beyond work without turning friendship into a sales process.  |

A strong demonstration message would be:

> “Hi Chris—last time we spoke, you were thinking about moving in the fall. Is that still the plan, or has the timing changed?”

It is specific enough to show context, but leaves room for a real conversation.

### How your planned AI, voice, and QR features would fit

Based on your earlier product direction, these are promising **roadmap applications**, not features I verified as live:

**Voice capture:** After an appointment, Maya could dictate the key context and ask for a proposed next step—then review it.

**AI assistance:** Help her turn a vague intention into an appropriate cadence or rewrite a message in her own tone.

**QR intake:** Give a new connection a clear, user-initiated way to share their information and the purpose of the exchange. Keep that purpose explicit rather than treating one interaction as permission for every future campaign.

For this persona, the best AI pitch is **less planning and typing**, not “let a bot manage your relationships.”

## What would make her try, buy, and keep using it?

### The event that makes the problem urgent

Her strongest purchase trigger is a recognizable failure—not a general desire to become more productive.

For example, she discovers that someone she meant to follow up with has moved forward with another agent. Or she finishes an event with several promising introductions and realizes none has an actual next step.

Other plausible triggers are changing brokerages, reviewing a neglected contact list, or deciding she needs a repeatable routine before her workload increases.

**The marketing opportunity is the moment she moves from “I should be more consistent” to “I need a different process.”**

### Her buying objections

| Objection                                    | What she needs to see                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **“I already have a CRM.”**                  | A demonstration of a specific follow-up problem the current setup is not solving. Do not assume access to a CRM means she has an effective daily habit. |
| **“This will become another app I ignore.”** | One useful outcome with a small set of real contacts—not a large setup project.                                                                         |
| **“I don’t want robotic messages.”**         | Context, editing, and her ability to decide what is sent.                                                                                               |
| **“Moving everything sounds exhausting.”**   | A way to start small. Do not promise automatic synchronization with her existing tools unless it is supported.                                          |
| **“I’m worried about my contacts.”**         | A clear explanation of data access, storage, permissions, and deletion—not vague assurances.                                                            |
| **“Is it worth paying for?”**                | Evidence from her own use: less preparation, more completed follow-ups, or fewer forgotten commitments.                                                 |

There is an existing technology-spending market to investigate: NAR’s 2025 survey found that **34% of respondents spent $50–$250 monthly on technology for their individual real estate businesses**. That makes paid adoption plausible, but does not establish willingness to pay for another tool. ([National Association of REALTORS®][3])

Your proposed **$49.99 AI/voice tier** should therefore be tested against demonstrated additional value. Maya may be a strong user of the core app without needing the highest tier.

### What would keep her subscribed

The strongest retention hypothesis is that she develops a repeatable routine: review a manageable set of people, complete relevant follow-ups, and leave knowing the next steps are accounted for.

The corresponding churn risk is that the app becomes **another database or another overdue-task list**.

## How I would market specifically to Maya

### Positioning

> **Jump in the Mix helps independent professionals turn good conversations into consistent, personal follow-through.**

For the real estate campaign:

> **Stay connected with the people behind your business—without making every conversation feel like a campaign.**

The first sells the broader product. The second speaks directly to the persona’s tension.

### Three message angles to test

| Angle                          | Example headline                                                           | What it tests                                                              |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Forgotten follow-through**   | **“You had a great conversation. Give it a next step.”**                   | Whether the immediate problem is losing momentum after meeting someone.    |
| **Personal communication**     | **“Stay in touch without sounding like a sales sequence.”**                | Whether authenticity is the strongest reason to choose the product.        |
| **Less daily decision-making** | **“Spend less time deciding who to contact. Spend more time connecting.”** | Whether planning and message preparation are the main sources of friction. |

Avoid leading with phrases such as “omnichannel orchestration,” “supercharge your pipeline,” or “put relationships on autopilot.” Those may describe a different product category—or a different buyer.

### Creative direction

Show an ordinary, recognizable moment: Maya finishes an appointment, pauses at a coffee shop, or sits down at her home desk.

The demonstration should move through **one person, one piece of context, and one next action**. The visible reward is a completed follow-up, not a dashboard full of activity.

Use warm, approachable visuals and actual product interaction. Keep the musical vocabulary as a memorable brand layer, but explain the practical benefit before asking someone to understand “Mixes” and “Beats.”

### Where to test acquisition

**Start with professional context and intent, not Maya’s fictional hobbies.**

| Channel hypothesis                            | Practical test                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Brokerage training and small agent groups** | Demonstrate a “not ready yet” prospect workflow and invite participants to try it with their own contacts.               |
| **Agent-focused educators and communities**   | Partner on a practical follow-up session or template walkthrough, rather than a generic sponsorship.                     |
| **Short social demonstrations**               | Test one recognizable problem per video: an old introduction, a postponed move, or a neglected past client.              |
| **Search-intent content**                     | Create useful material around follow-up routines, staying in touch with past clients, and simple contact follow-through. |
| **Peer invitations**                          | Encourage satisfied users to share a specific useful workflow with another independent professional.                     |

Social content is a reasonable channel to test: NAR’s technology survey reported social media use by 75% of respondents. That supports experimentation—not an assumption that a particular platform or ad format will convert. ([National Association of REALTORS®][3])

For now, campaign calls to action should match your public access model: **try the demo or join the free-account waitlist**, rather than promise unrestricted immediate signup. ([Jump in the Mix][1])

## The targeting criteria that actually matter

Maya’s car, food preferences, and family structure make her easier to picture. These are the factors I would use to identify promising customers:

| Strong-fit signal                                               | Why it matters                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Their income depends meaningfully on relationships.**         | Follow-through has a clear professional purpose.                                     |
| **They already have people worth staying in touch with.**       | The app can improve an existing situation rather than wait for a network to develop. |
| **They personally handle communication.**                       | The user directly experiences both the problem and the benefit.                      |
| **They can describe recent missed or delayed follow-ups.**      | There is observable pain, not just abstract interest in productivity.                |
| **Their current process is fragmented or inconsistently used.** | There is room for a simpler routine.                                                 |
| **They value personal communication over message volume.**      | This aligns with the proposed positioning.                                           |
| **They are willing to spend a few minutes following through.**  | Planning assistance only helps when the user completes the actions.                  |

**Lower-priority customers for the first campaign:** people who primarily want bulk cold outreach, teams seeking a complete operational CRM replacement, professionals already satisfied with a well-run follow-up system, and casual users without a recurring need.

Consultants, coaches, and high-value local service providers are sensible comparison segments. I would test them alongside real estate before declaring a permanent winner.

## Recommended validation

Recruit a small research group of solo agents who match the behavioral criteria, including a range of ages and business sizes. Ask them to walk through a **recent real example** of missed follow-up, show their current workaround, and try the app with a few relevant contacts.

Watch whether they return and complete follow-ups—not just whether they like the concept. Compare that behavior with a smaller group of consultants or service-business owners. Use actual upgrade decisions to test pricing.

**The most useful ICP summary is this:**

> **A relationship-driven independent professional whose network is too valuable to neglect, whose communication is too personal to turn into bulk outreach, and whose business is too small to have someone else manage follow-through.**

That is the customer condition to build around. **Maya is one believable expression of it—not the boundary of your market.**
