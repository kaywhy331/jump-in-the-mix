## 8. His highest-value Jump in the Mix use cases

The following are **proposed consulting workflows**, not claims that dedicated consulting templates or integrations already exist.

| Situation                                                   | Proposed workflow                                                                 | Value to Daniel                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A prospective client says “after our next hiring round”** | Record the stated milestone and plan a relevant check-in around it.               | Keeps an opportunity from disappearing without pressuring the buyer prematurely. |
| **A professional introduction arrives**                     | Follow through with the new person and acknowledge the introducer.                | Protects trust on both sides of the introduction.                                |

| **A past client’s priorities change**                       | Reconnect around something relevant to the relationship.                          | Opens a conversation without pretending there is a current need.                 |
| **A referral partner goes quiet**                           | Share an appropriate update, resource, or invitation to catch up.                 | Maintains professional reciprocity.                                              |
| **He meets someone at a workshop**                          | Capture the topic discussed and follow through on any promised resource.          | Turns a pleasant conversation into a useful professional connection.             |

### The strongest demonstration: “Not now” becomes a real next step

**Context:** A business owner liked Daniel’s approach but wanted to finish hiring before discussing onboarding improvements.

**Example message:**

> “Hi Jordan—when we spoke, you wanted to revisit onboarding once the new managers were in place. Has that timing changed, or would it be useful to pick up the conversation?”

The value is not simply generating a sentence. It is keeping the relationship, context, and timing connected.

### A second example: post-project continuity

> “Hi Priya—it’s been a few weeks since we handed over the new intake process. What’s working well, and where is the team still getting stuck?”

That gives Daniel a reason to be useful before asking for more work.

**Cadence principle:** begin with the timing discussed with the other person. After a reply, changed priority, or request to stop, revise the plan. Do not market a universal sequence of repeated nudges as the answer to every relationship.

### Strong-fit signals

| **Will complete the follow-ups**                        | A planning tool cannot substitute for willingness to communicate.           |

### Lower-priority fits

A consultant with no network and no defined offer has a different primary problem. So does someone seeking mass cold outreach, an enterprise sales team needing extensive account coordination, or a professional already satisfied with a well-maintained follow-up process.

### The biggest risks in this segment

**Low usage frequency:** A small network may not justify daily activity. Evaluate whether the app supports the right routine, rather than demanding daily engagement.

**Overlap with existing tools:** Daniel may already have a workable setup. The app must reduce effort—not merely relocate information.

**Expectation creep:** Consultants may ask for proposals, billing, project delivery, or extensive integrations. Those requests should not automatically redefine the product.

**Episodic adoption:** He may use the app only when an engagement ends. The more important retention test is whether he keeps using it while fully occupied.

## Recommended validation

Recruit established solo consultants with warm networks and recent examples of delayed follow-up. Include different specialties and ages rather than treating Daniel’s biography as a screening checklist.

Have them work with actual contacts, then observe use across both busy and quieter weeks. Look for completed follow-ups, relevant next steps, manageable upkeep, and voluntary return. Use actual upgrade behavior to evaluate the paid proposition.

**The clearest summary of ICP #2 is:**

> **An established independent expert whose next opportunity is likely to involve someone they already know—but whose current client work keeps pushing those relationships out of view.**

For Maya, the central promise is **dependable personal attention**.

For Daniel, it is **maintaining future-business relationships without letting them take over the workday**.
